## Pregnancy danger signs, go to hospital now

Any of these in pregnancy or in the six weeks after delivery needs immediate care. Call **102** or **108** for a maternal ambulance, or **112**:

- Vaginal bleeding at any stage of pregnancy
- Severe headache with blurred vision, flashing lights, or swelling of the face and hands, which may indicate pre-eclampsia
- A fit or convulsion
- Severe abdominal pain
- High fever with or without chills
- Reduced or absent fetal movement after 28 weeks, compared with the usual daily pattern
- Fluid leaking from the vagina before 37 weeks
- Severe breathlessness, chest pain, or a swollen painful calf
- Persistent vomiting with inability to keep fluids down
- Heavy bleeding, foul-smelling discharge, or fever after delivery

Pre-eclampsia is often silent until it is advanced, which is why every antenatal visit includes a blood pressure check and a urine test for protein. Do not skip visits because you feel well.

## Routine antenatal care in India

The national programme recommends at least four antenatal visits, with the first as early as possible in the first trimester. Care usually includes registration and an MCP card, weight and blood pressure at every visit, blood tests for haemoglobin, blood group, blood sugar, HIV, syphilis and hepatitis B, at least one ultrasound, iron and folic acid supplementation, calcium supplementation, and two tetanus-diphtheria doses. Free services, transport and delivery care are available through Janani Suraksha Yojana and Janani Shishu Suraksha Karyakram at government facilities.

Folic acid taken from before conception through the first trimester substantially reduces the risk of neural tube defects. Anaemia is very common in Indian pregnancies and is worth treating actively, since it raises the risk of preterm birth and postpartum haemorrhage.

## Newborn danger signs

A newborn can deteriorate within hours. Seek emergency care if a baby under two months has any of:

- Difficulty feeding, or refusing to feed
- Fast breathing, chest indrawing, grunting, or pauses in breathing
- Fever, or a temperature that is unusually low with cold hands and feet
- Convulsions or abnormal movements
- Reduced movement, floppiness, or being difficult to wake
- Yellow discolouration of the palms and soles, or jaundice appearing in the first 24 hours
- A red, swollen or discharging umbilical stump
- Fewer than six wet nappies a day after the first week

**Any fever in an infant under three months is a medical emergency until proven otherwise.** Do not wait and watch.

Exclusive breastfeeding is recommended for the first six months, starting within the first hour after birth, with complementary foods added at six months while breastfeeding continues. Skin-to-skin contact, keeping the baby warm, and clean cord care are the simplest measures that save newborn lives.

## Danger signs in older infants and children

Take a child to a doctor urgently if you see:

- Fast or laboured breathing, or the chest sucking in with each breath
- Refusal to drink, or vomiting everything
- Lethargy, unusual drowsiness, or difficulty waking
- A convulsion
- A rash that does not fade when pressed with a glass
- No urine for 8 hours, sunken eyes, or a skin pinch that stays up
- Severe or persistent abdominal pain
- A stiff neck, or a bulging soft spot on the head
- Any fever in a child under three months

Diarrhoea and pneumonia remain leading causes of child death in India, and both are treatable when caught early. The two interventions that matter most at home are ORS with zinc for diarrhoea, and recognising fast breathing as a sign of pneumonia that needs assessment rather than home remedies.

## Childhood immunisation

India Universal Immunisation Programme provides free vaccines at government facilities and outreach sessions, protecting against tuberculosis, polio, diphtheria, pertussis, tetanus, hepatitis B, Haemophilus influenzae type b, rotavirus, pneumococcal disease, measles and rubella, and Japanese encephalitis in endemic districts.

Keep the immunisation card safe and bring it to every visit. If a dose is missed, the schedule can almost always be caught up rather than restarted. Mild fever, irritability or soreness at the injection site for a day or two is expected. High fever that persists, a fit, or a large spreading swelling should be reported.
