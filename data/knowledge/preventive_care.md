## Which health checks are worth having

Screening is only useful when it changes what happens next. These are the checks with the strongest evidence for adults in India:

| Check | Who | How often |
|---|---|---|
| Blood pressure | All adults | At least yearly from age 18 |
| Blood sugar, fasting or HbA1c | All adults from 30, earlier with family history or high waist | Every 1 to 3 years |
| Lipid profile | Adults from 30 to 40, or earlier with risk factors | Every 3 to 5 years |
| Weight and waist circumference | All adults | At every visit |
| Haemoglobin | Adolescent girls, women of reproductive age, pregnancy | Periodically |
| Oral, breast and cervical examination | Adults from 30, under the national NCD programme | Every 5 years, free at government facilities |
| Cervical cancer screening | Women 30 to 65 | Every 5 years with HPV testing, or as advised |
| Vision and hearing | Adults from 40, and all children | Periodically |
| Dental review | All ages | Yearly |

Whole-body scans and large unfocused test panels marketed as executive health packages are not recommended. They generate incidental findings that lead to anxiety and further tests without improving outcomes.

## Vaccines for adults

Vaccination is not only for children. Depending on age, occupation, pregnancy and health conditions, discuss: annual influenza vaccine, tetanus and diphtheria boosters every ten years, hepatitis B for healthcare workers and those at risk, HPV vaccine for adolescents and young adults, pneumococcal and shingles vaccines for older adults and those with chronic disease, rabies vaccination after any animal bite or scratch, and typhoid vaccine in high-risk settings. Anyone who has had their spleen removed, or who takes immunosuppressive treatment, needs a specific vaccination plan.

After any dog, cat or monkey bite or scratch that breaks the skin, wash the wound with soap under running water for 15 minutes and seek rabies vaccination the same day. Rabies is almost always fatal once symptoms begin, and it is entirely preventable before then.

## Eating well on an Indian plate

- Fill half the plate with vegetables and fruit, a quarter with whole grains such as millets, brown rice or whole wheat, and a quarter with protein from dal, rajma, chana, eggs, fish, chicken, paneer or soya.
- Include pulses daily. Most Indian diets are cereal-heavy and protein-light.
- Keep added sugar low. Sugary drinks, packaged juices and sweets are the easiest calories to cut with no loss of nutrition.
- Keep salt under 5 grams a day, roughly one level teaspoon from all sources including pickles, papad and packaged snacks.
- Use a variety of cooking oils in moderation and avoid repeatedly reheated frying oil. Avoid partially hydrogenated fats such as vanaspati.
- Prefer whole fruit over juice, and nuts and roasted chana over fried snacks.
- Iron from plant sources absorbs better with vitamin C in the same meal, for example lemon over dal. Tea and coffee with meals reduce iron absorption, so keep them between meals.
- Anaemia is very common in India, particularly in adolescent girls and women. Persistent tiredness, breathlessness on exertion, pale nails or hair fall deserve a haemoglobin test rather than a supplement bought on a hunch.

## Movement, sleep and tobacco

WHO recommends at least **150 to 300 minutes of moderate activity a week**, or 75 to 150 minutes of vigorous activity, plus muscle-strengthening on two or more days. Any movement beats none, and breaking up long sitting matters independently of exercise sessions. A 10-minute walk after each meal has a measurable effect on blood sugar.

Adults generally need seven to nine hours of sleep. Persistent insomnia, loud snoring with pauses in breathing, or daytime sleepiness that affects driving should be assessed rather than endured.

Tobacco in every form is harmful, including bidi, khaini, gutkha, zarda, paan masala with tobacco and hookah. Smokeless tobacco is the leading cause of oral cancer in India. Quitting brings benefits within weeks, and support is free on the National Tobacco Quitline **1800-11-2356**. Most people who quit successfully have made several previous attempts, so a relapse is a step in the process rather than a failure.

Alcohol has no safe level for cancer risk. If you drink, keeping it infrequent and modest, with several alcohol-free days a week, reduces harm.

## When to see a doctor even if you feel fine

Book an appointment for unintended weight loss, a lump anywhere, a sore or ulcer that has not healed in three weeks, a change in bowel or bladder habit lasting more than a few weeks, bleeding from any site that is not explained, a cough lasting more than two weeks, a mole that changes shape or colour, persistent hoarseness, difficulty swallowing, or unusual fatigue that does not improve with rest. None of these means something serious is present, but all of them are worth checking early, when the range of options is widest.
