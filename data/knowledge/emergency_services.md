## When to call emergency services immediately

Call **112** (national all-in-one emergency number) or **108** (ambulance in most states) right away if a person has any of the following:

- Chest pain or pressure lasting more than a few minutes, especially with sweating, nausea, or pain spreading to the arm, jaw, neck or back
- Sudden weakness or numbness of the face, arm or leg, especially on one side of the body
- Sudden confusion, trouble speaking, or trouble understanding speech
- Severe difficulty breathing, or breathing so fast the person cannot speak a full sentence
- Unconsciousness, a seizure that does not stop, or a person who cannot be woken
- Heavy bleeding that does not stop with firm pressure
- A serious injury after a road accident, a fall from height, or a head injury with vomiting or drowsiness
- Suspected poisoning, snakebite, drowning, electrocution, or a large burn
- Blue or grey lips, tongue or fingertips

Do not drive yourself to hospital if you have chest pain or stroke symptoms. Call for an ambulance so that treatment can begin on the way.

## Key Indian helpline numbers

| Service | Number |
|---|---|
| National emergency (police, fire, ambulance) | 112 |
| Ambulance (most states) | 108 |
| Maternal and child health ambulance | 102 |
| National health helpline | 104 |
| Mental health support (Tele-MANAS) | 14416 |
| Mental health support (KIRAN) | 1800-599-0019 |
| Poison information (NPIC, AIIMS New Delhi) | 1800-116-117 |
| Child helpline | 1098 |
| Women helpline | 181 |
| Senior citizen helpline | 14567 |

Numbers can vary slightly by state. If one number does not connect, dial **112**.

## What to tell the emergency operator

Stay calm and give information in this order so help is not delayed:

1. Exact location, with a landmark and, if possible, a pin location shared on the phone
2. What happened, in one sentence
3. Whether the person is conscious and breathing
4. Age and sex of the person, and any known conditions such as diabetes, heart disease or pregnancy
5. Your callback number

Stay on the line until the operator tells you to hang up. Send someone to the gate or roadside to guide the ambulance in.

## While waiting for the ambulance

- Keep the person still, warm and reassured. Do not give food or water to someone who is drowsy, confused or may need surgery.
- If the person is unconscious but breathing normally, place them on their side in the recovery position.
- If they are not breathing and you are trained, start chest compressions: push hard and fast in the centre of the chest, about 100 to 120 compressions per minute.
- Collect medicines, prescriptions, prior discharge summaries and any government health card to carry with you.
