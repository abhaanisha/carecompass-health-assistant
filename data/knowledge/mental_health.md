## If you are in crisis right now

If you are thinking about ending your life, or about harming yourself, please reach out to a person right now. These lines are free, confidential, and staffed by people trained to listen:

| Service | Number | Hours |
|---|---|---|
| Tele-MANAS, Government of India | 14416 or 1800-891-4416 | 24x7, many Indian languages |
| KIRAN, Ministry of Social Justice | 1800-599-0019 | 24x7, 13 languages |
| Vandrevala Foundation | 9999 666 555 | 24x7 |
| AASRA | 9820 466 726 | 24x7 |
| Emergency services | 112 | 24x7 |

If someone is in immediate physical danger, has taken an overdose, or has injured themselves, call **112** or go to the nearest emergency department now. Stay with them. Move medicines, pesticides, sharp objects and other means of harm out of reach. You do not need to have the right words. Staying present and listening without judgement matters more than anything you say.

Thoughts of suicide are a sign of pain that has become overwhelming, not of weakness or failure. They are common, they are treatable, and they pass with the right support.

## Understanding stress, anxiety and low mood

Feeling anxious before an exam, an interview or a medical result is a normal response. It becomes a health problem when it is persistent, out of proportion, and starts interfering with sleep, work, studies or relationships.

Common features of an anxiety problem: constant worry that is hard to control, restlessness, irritability, poor concentration, muscle tension, disturbed sleep, and physical symptoms such as palpitations, chest tightness, breathlessness, stomach upset or dizziness. Panic attacks bring a sudden surge of intense fear with racing heart, shaking, sweating, tingling and a feeling of losing control. They peak within about ten minutes and are frightening but not physically dangerous. That said, a first episode of chest pain or breathlessness should always be assessed medically before it is assumed to be anxiety.

Common features of depression: low mood or loss of interest and pleasure for most of the day, nearly every day, for two weeks or more, plus changes in sleep and appetite, fatigue, difficulty concentrating, feelings of worthlessness or guilt, and in some cases thoughts of death. In India, depression frequently presents physically, as body aches, weakness, headache or burning sensations, rather than as reported sadness.

## What helps

Evidence-based self-management, useful alongside professional care rather than instead of it:

- **Sleep regularity.** A fixed wake time, daylight exposure in the morning, and no screens for the last hour do more than most sleep aids.
- **Physical activity.** Around 30 minutes of moderate exercise most days has an effect size comparable to some treatments for mild to moderate depression.
- **Structure.** When motivation is low, schedule small, specific activities rather than waiting to feel like doing them. Action usually precedes motivation, not the other way around.
- **Breathing and grounding.** Slow breathing with a longer out-breath than in-breath, for a few minutes, reliably settles acute anxiety. The 5-4-3-2-1 grounding exercise helps in a panic attack: name five things you can see, four you can touch, three you can hear, two you can smell, one you can taste.
- **Limit alcohol, cannabis and heavy caffeine.** All of them worsen anxiety and sleep, even when they seem to help in the moment.
- **Connection.** Telling one trusted person is consistently one of the strongest protective factors.

## When to seek professional help

See a doctor, psychologist or psychiatrist if symptoms have lasted more than two weeks, are worsening, affect your ability to work or study, involve panic attacks, or involve any thought of self-harm. Also seek help for hearing voices, unusual beliefs others find alarming, prolonged inability to sleep, or a marked change in behaviour noticed by family.

Treatment works. Talking therapies such as cognitive behavioural therapy, medication, or a combination, help most people. Antidepressants are not addictive, usually take two to four weeks to show benefit, and should never be stopped abruptly without medical advice. In India, district hospitals run mental health clinics under the National Mental Health Programme, Tele-MANAS offers free teleconsultation, and many medical colleges provide low-cost psychiatry outpatient care.

## Supporting someone else

Ask directly and calmly. Asking someone whether they are thinking of suicide does not plant the idea; it usually brings relief. Listen without rushing to fix, reassure, or minimise. Avoid advice like just be positive or others have it worse. Help them take one practical step, such as booking an appointment or making the call together. Keep checking in over the following weeks, since risk does not end when a single conversation does. Look after your own limits as well, and do not promise secrecy when someone safety is at stake.
